Three boards for a combat robot

Before I knew I wanted to do RTL, I was on the electrical team at Purdue RoboMaster drawing schematics and laying out PCBs. Three boards came out of it. One got fabricated, soldered, and tested; the other two stopped at a finished layout.

// 01 — audio visualizer

audio_visualizer fabricated + working

This was part of the onboarding project, and it was the one that taught me the most about circuit and PCB design. This product uses an LM3915 LED driver, a microphone and different op-amps to amplify the input audio, resulting in the LED lighting up.

// 02 — armor power system

armor_plate_v1 PCB designed, not fabricated

A rechargeable power system for the robot’s armor plates. The armor modules take hits and have to keep reporting, so the board’s job is delivering stable power to them without a harness running back to the main battery. Designed and routed, but was never used since the team decided that they did not need it anymore.

// what I took from it

Board-level design is where I learned that a design is not done when the schematic is done. On the one board I actually built, footprints were wrong and the first revision did not behave, and I only found out by probing it. That habit — assume it is broken until measured — is the same one I now bring to RTL and to simulator correlation work.
